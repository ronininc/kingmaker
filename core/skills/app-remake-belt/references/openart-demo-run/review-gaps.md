# Review Gaps: OpenArt (Entrepreneur agent)

**Honest count:** this is snippet-level, not a full read. Excerpts from 5 result pages of Trustpilot's OpenArt listing (site shows roughly 448-598 reviews depending on the page snapshot; a TrustScore of 3.5/5 appeared on one) plus one third-party review's complaints section. Public reviews only. Roughly 25-30 individual review excerpts seen. A full pass needs the complete review set read, with dates, before anything is claimed.

## Recurring complaints (seen in multiple excerpts)
1. **Credits burn too fast.** "Most expensive platform," 12k credits with no usable result, a 3-minute music video reported around 6,000 credits.
2. **Editing costs extra.** Fixing an AI mistake can cost more than redoing the image; text removal reported at 450 credits and then failing.
3. **Credits expire / don't roll over** month to month.
4. **Credits removed mid-cycle on plan change** (one reviewer: 45,000 credits wiped after activating a new subscription).
5. **Costly accidental double-spends,** e.g. paying twice to train a character.
6. **Failed or bad generations still consume credits;** refund handling inconsistent (some praise quick refunds, others say credits never restored).
7. **Support is hit-or-miss;** some praise it, others say it never replies.
8. **Strict cancellation / accidental subscriptions,** lock-in fear.
9. **UI annoyance:** a promo banner covering the credit balance.

## What the remake can do differently (the sellable edge)
- Show cost before spend and a live, visible credit/usage ledger.
- Auto-refund on failed generations.
- No expiry on what the user owns; no mid-cycle wipes.
- Confirm before re-charging an identical expensive action (character training).
- Cheap edits: mask/inpaint on local models costs compute, not credit multiples.
- Under belt rule 5, $0 new spend: local open models mean no per-image credits at all.

## Caveats
- Trustpilot skews to extremes; positive reviews also appear (fast generation, model variety).
- Summaries and promo-linked articles are not primary evidence.

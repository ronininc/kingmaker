# Spoken commands, by kind

Say the wake word first (or wake Kingmaker another way), then talk normally. These are examples, not a fixed list:
Kingmaker takes ordinary speech.

## Kingmaker itself

(`kingmaker status` in a terminal shows how many proposals are waiting for your word.)

- "What can you drive?"
- "Stop approving."
- "That's all." / "Go to sleep."
- "Shut down."

## The day

- "What's on my calendar today, and does anything conflict?"
- "What should I do first?"
- "What's blocked?"
- "Summarize what changed since yesterday."

## Messages and mail

- "Summarize the latest email from the landlord, then draft a reply."
- "Draft a message saying I'll be ten minutes late." (prepared, not sent)
- "Send the draft." (read back, then it waits for your yes)
- "Who hasn't answered me this week?"

## Calendar

- "Find a free half hour tomorrow afternoon."
- "Put a review on Friday at three." (proposed; your yes adds it)
- "Prepare tomorrow's agenda."

## Files and documents

- "Find the newest version of the price sheet."
- "Compare these two documents and tell me what changed."
- "Make a short report from these notes."

## Apps on the Mac

- "Open Notes and start a new note." (the click waits for your yes)
- "Open bbc.com." (opens that page in Chrome)
- "PhotoCraft, make a square image from beach.jpg with the words Summer sale." (a job that writes a file waits for
  your yes)

## Research

- "What changed in the latest macOS release? Show me the sources."
- "What do the analysts think of a ticker?" (research only, never a trade)

## Code

- "Summarize the changes in this repository since yesterday."
- "Prepare a fix for this bug." (prepared; committing waits for your yes)

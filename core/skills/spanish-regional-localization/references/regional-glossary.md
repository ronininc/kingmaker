# Regional Spanish Glossary — Starter Reference

Starting point only. Verify anything going into permanent copy against live sources (SKILL.md Step 3). Organized by region, then by category: **Warmth/greetings**, **Ordering & CTAs**, **Money**, **Everyday food words**, **Known traps** (words that mean something different — or something vulgar — elsewhere).

---

## Mexico — Sinaloa / Sonora (coastal-norteño)

Signals: mariscos, aguachile, mazatleco/culichi-style seafood, carne asada culture.

- **Warmth**: "compa," "carnal" (buddy — casual, use sparingly in copy), "qué buena onda" (how great)
- **Ordering**: "ándele, pida ya" (go on, order now) reads natural here; "aquí le entramos con..." (here we serve up...) is a common local food-vendor phrasing
- **Money**: "feria," "varo" (informal for money/cash — casual register, fine for a playful CTA, not for formal site copy)
- **Food**: "aguachile," "chocolata" (a Sinaloa clam variety), "machaca," "chilorio"
- **Traps**: heavy narco-slang associated with this region online (from media/TV) should be avoided entirely — it reads as a joke or worse, not as authentic regional pride

## Mexico — Chihuahua / border

Signals: burritos culture (burritos are more central here than in southern Mexico), norteño beef/ranching identity.

- **Warmth**: "compa," more clipped/direct phrasing generally than central Mexican Spanish
- **Food**: "burrito" (a staple, not a novelty item here), "machaca," "discada"
- **Traps**: don't default to Chihuahua-style vocabulary for a Sinaloa/Sonora seafood business or vice versa — border ranching register and coastal mariscos register read differently to locals even though both are "norteño"

## Mexico — Central (CDMX / Puebla / general "standard" Mexican)

This is the register most general-purpose Spanish training data defaults toward — good default ONLY if the client is actually from this region, not a safe universal fallback for all Mexican clients.

- **Warmth**: "qué padre," "está padrísimo," "órale"
- **Ordering**: "haz tu pedido," "ordena ahora"
- **Food**: "antojitos," "tacos de canasta," "elote" (not "choclo")
- **Traps**: "popote" for drinking straw is central-Mexican; doesn't transfer to the Caribbean or much of South America

## Mexico — Yucatán

Signals: cochinita pibil, relleno negro, Mayan-inflected dish names, achiote-based cooking.

- **Food**: dish names often keep Maya-derived spelling/pronunciation — don't "correct" or simplify these
- **Warmth**: generally softer/more formal cadence than norteño Spanish; less use of "órale"-type interjections

## Cuba

- **Warmth**: "asere," "qué bolá" (very informal — greeting-only register, not for formal CTAs), "mi amor/mi cielo" used more freely and warmly in customer-facing copy than in most other regions
- **Ordering**: "coge tu pedido," "manda tu orden" reads more natural than the more neutral "realiza tu pedido"
- **Money**: "fula" (informal, avoid in site copy), "chavos" is Puerto Rican not Cuban — don't cross-contaminate
- **Food**: "ropa vieja," "mojo," "ajiaco," "tostones," "frita" (Cuban burger) — keep as-is, don't generalize
- **Traps**: "guagua" = bus in Cuba (also PR/Canary Islands) — NOT "baby," which is the Chilean/Andean sense. "Fruta bomba" is the Cuban word for papaya (in much of Cuba "papaya" is vulgar slang — a real trap for a fruit-forward menu)

## Colombia

Already validated on real client sites.

- **Warmth**: "parce/parcero" (very casual, use sparingly), "qué chévere," "bacano"
- **Ordering**: "haz tu pedido," "pide ya" both read natural
- **Food**: "picada," "calentado," "arepa," "bandeja paisa," "jugos naturales" — confirmed working vocabulary from real client copy
- **Traps**: avoid literal calques from English business-speak — e.g. "bien raíz digital" for "digital real estate" or "avenida de ingresos" for "revenue avenue" read as translated-from-English, not natural. Prefer "presencia digital," "canal de ingresos" (the house standard)

## Venezuela

Signals: arepas, cachapas, tequeños, pabellón criollo.

- **Warmth**: "chamo/chama" (informal for guy/girl), "qué pana" (how cool/great — "pana" also just means friend)
- **Food**: "arepa," "cachapa," "tequeños," "reina pepiada" (a specific arepa filling — keep as-is)
- **Traps**: "guayoyo" (Venezuelan light-brew coffee) is a real, specific term — don't generalize to just "café"

## Puerto Rico

- **Warmth**: "boricua" (self-identifier, use with care/only if the business itself uses it), "qué chévere," "brutal" (as in "great," not literally brutal)
- **Money**: "chavos" (money — PR-specific, don't reuse in Cuba/DR copy)
- **Food**: "mofongo," "tostones," "alcapurrias," "pastelillos"

## Dominican Republic

- **Warmth**: "tíguere" (a specific DR term for a sharp/streetwise person — casual, situational), "qué lo que" (very informal greeting)
- **Food**: "mangú," "la bandera," "tostones," "chimichurri" (DR chimichurri = a sandwich, NOT the Argentine sauce — real trap if the site also references Argentine-style chimichurri)

## Argentina / Río de la Plata

- **Warmth**: "che," "qué copado/a," uses "vos" instead of "tú" — this changes verb conjugation across the whole site's copy, not just individual words (e.g. "pedí ahora" not "pide ahora")
- **Food**: "asado," "choripán," "chimichurri" (the sauce, here), "medialunas"
- **Traps**: "vos" conjugation is the single biggest tell — a site using "tú" forms throughout will read as non-Argentine even if the vocabulary is otherwise right

| guagua | — (not common) | bus (Cuba, PR) | baby (Chile, parts of Andes) |

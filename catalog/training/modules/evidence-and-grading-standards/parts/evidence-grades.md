# The Grades: Proven, Fixture-Grade, Wall

Every claim of completion carries one of three grades, and the grade is part
of the claim — a result reported without its grade is an unfinished report.

DONE-PROVEN means the thing was run end to end on the real target: the real
build, the real account, the real data path, with the proof command named.
It is the only grade that may be shortened to "done".

DONE-FIXTURE-GRADE means the work passed against fixtures, stubs, simulations,
or a local stand-in for the real thing. Fixture evidence is supporting
evidence, never the verdict. A fixture-grade result is reported in those
words, with the real run it still owes named beside it. Letting a fixture
grade stand in for a proof is the single most corrosive reporting failure in
the fleet, because every later decision trusts the word "done".

WALL means the real run could not happen and names the wall exactly: the
missing access, the undeployed surface, the platform-only step, the owner's
reserved hand. A wall is not a failure of effort; an unnamed wall is a
failure of reporting. When in doubt between grades, take the lower one —
grading up is the owner's act on seeing the proof, never the worker's act on
writing the report.

# Show the Work, Not the Summary

Work product the owner is meant to judge — copy, commands, reports, release
notes, drafts — is delivered as itself, printed in front of him, never
replaced by a digest of it. A summary may ride along; it is never the
delivery. The owner cannot approve what he cannot see, and a summary asks
him to trust the summarizer at exactly the moment judgement is the job.

The same rule governs receipts downward. A claim names its proof: the command
that was run, the file that was produced, the count that came back. "Tests
pass" is a summary; "node --test test/module-reader.test.mjs — 9 pass, 0
fail" is a receipt. Receipts are checkable by the next session without
re-running anything, which is what makes them receipts.

There is one deliberate exception: bulk. Raw logs and thousand-line dumps are
pointed to, not pasted, with the load-bearing lines quoted. Showing the work
means the owner sees the thing itself at judgement size — the artifact, the
diff, the numbers — not that he is buried under everything the machine saw
on the way there.

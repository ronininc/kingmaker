# Legal paperwork lane — footprint reclamation

Purpose: turn a footprint finding into the strongest lawful filing available for
that holder, record type, and jurisdiction — then pursue it through confirmation,
appeal, regulator complaint, or counsel escalation. This is a document-preparation
and filing workflow, not a law firm and not a promise that every public record can
be erased.

## Guardrails

- **Subject and authority:** work only for the person whose records are involved,
  using their own authorization. For Robbie, the footprint-wipe owner-only rule
  applies. Any future client use needs its own signed authorization, identity
  verification, scope, and records-retention terms before work starts.
- **No legal representation:** the lane can organize facts, select standard
  instruments, prepare packets, calendar deadlines, and file administrative or
  platform requests where the subject's authorization permits it. It does not
  hold itself out as a lawyer. Eligibility for sealing, expungement, injunctions,
  defamation/unmasking actions, and contested public-record matters goes to a
  licensed attorney in the relevant jurisdiction, or becomes an explicitly
  owner-directed pro se packet after that limit is stated.
- **Verify at filing time:** statutes, forms, registries, response clocks, and
  broker procedures change. Confirm the current official instructions and legal
  basis before each filing; never rely on a remembered deadline, fee, form, or URL.
- **Data minimization:** send each recipient only the identifiers it needs to
  match and act on the request. Keep ID documents in a separate restricted packet;
  never place ID images, full government numbers, or unnecessary DOB/address
  history in the general wipe log.
- **ID-upload gate:** any filing requiring an identity-document upload remains a
  separate owner decision, exactly as in the core skill.

## Case intake

Create one case record per person and jurisdiction:

1. Legal name and every variant/handle to be reclaimed.
2. Current and historical contact identifiers relevant to matching: emails,
   phones, addresses, usernames, profile URLs, listing URLs, and screenshots.
3. Residency and relevant jurisdictional facts, without assuming a state's
   privacy statute applies.
4. Objective per record: disclose, delete, correct, suppress, opt out of sale or
   sharing, freeze, de-index, remove media, stop prescreening, or preserve status.
5. Proof package: screenshots/URLs showing the exposure, the holder's identity,
   and the harm or inaccuracy where correction/dispute law requires it.
6. Authority record: signed authorization, date, scope, and permitted channels.

## Instrument library

### 1. Consumer-privacy and data-broker rights requests

Use the holder's official privacy portal or designated rights channel for:

- access/know and consumer-disclosure requests;
- deletion, correction, and portability where the applicable law provides them;
- opt out of sale, sharing, targeted advertising, and profiling;
- limit use/disclosure of sensitive personal information;
- data-broker suppression and do-not-sell listings;
- authorized-agent filings where the jurisdiction permits an agent and the
  holder's verification rules are satisfied.

Candidate regimes to test for eligibility at run time include California,
Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Minnesota,
Montana, Nebraska, New Jersey, New Hampshire, Oregon, Rhode Island, Tennessee,
Texas, Utah, and Virginia privacy laws; other state/sector laws may also apply.
Arizona has no single comprehensive consumer-privacy statute assumed by this
lane, so for an Arizona subject the workflow must identify another applicable
law, a broker's national rights process, a voluntary suppression channel, or a
sector-specific remedy instead of inventing a statutory basis.

Also check official data-broker registries and centralized deletion/opt-out
systems in every jurisdiction where the subject qualifies. Registry membership
is evidence a business claims broker status — it is not a substitute for filing
with the actual holder shown in the footprint report.

### 2. Consumer-reporting and screening records (FCRA lane)

Where the holder is a consumer reporting agency or specialty screening company:

- obtain the consumer disclosure/file to see what is actually held;
- dispute incomplete or inaccurate information with supporting evidence;
- request reinvestigation and correction/deletion of information that cannot be
  verified;
- security freezes and fraud alerts where appropriate;
- opt out of prescreened credit and insurance offers through the official
  prescreen channel;
- cover the specialty bureaus relevant to the footprint: credit, tenant,
  employment, banking/ChexSystems, utilities, insurance claims, and LexisNexis
  or similar consumer files.

Do not label every broker an FCRA bureau. First classify how the holder uses the
data; the remedy follows that classification.

### 3. Search, platform, and media filings

- Google and Bing personal-information, outdated-page, and imagery removal
  processes, beginning with source removal where possible.
- DMCA notices only for material the subject owns or is authorized to enforce;
  identify the work, the infringing URL, ownership, and a good-faith statement.
  Never use copyright paperwork to suppress material merely because it is
  unflattering or belongs to someone else.
- Platform privacy, impersonation, non-consensual imagery, harassment, and
  right-of-publicity reporting channels, with preservation copies retained by
  the subject before requesting removal.
- News, review, mugshot, and republishing sites: use their stated correction or
  suppression process where one exists; escalate false statements of fact,
  extortionate pay-for-removal schemes, and refusals to counsel/regulators.

### 4. Communications, mail, and advertising suppression

- National Do Not Call registration and company-specific do-not-call requests.
- Direct-mail and email preference services, catalog suppression, and the
  holder's own global privacy controls.
- Carrier, mobile advertising ID, connected-TV, and app-tracking controls when
  they form part of the footprint case. These are account controls, not court
  filings; log them separately so the report does not overstate what was filed.

### 5. Government and court records

Broker and platform forms do not erase truthful government, news, or court
records. Put these on the **legal-residue docket** and route each item to its
actual channel:

- correction/amendment requests for the subject's own agency records, including
  Privacy Act or state-equivalent requests where available;
- court-record sealing, expungement, set-aside, or confidentiality petitions
  only after jurisdiction-specific eligibility review by counsel, or as an
  owner-directed pro se packet using the court's current official forms;
- voter, property, licensing, and agency confidentiality programs where the
  subject qualifies;
- FOIA/public-records requests to learn what an agency holds or disclosed,
  understanding that FOIA is an access tool, not a general deletion right;
- protective orders, injunctions, and litigation holds only through counsel.

For every residue item record: custodian, record identifier, public source,
possible remedy, eligibility question, likely filing office, fee verified at run
time, expected review path, and the decision needed from the owner/counsel.

### 7. Government systems — elective exit, reduction, or mandatory stay

Government exposure is not one kind of record. Before preparing any filing,
classify the system using `GOVERNMENT-SYSTEMS.md`:

- **EXIT:** a voluntary enrollment, account, registration, or program that the
  subject can withdraw from, cancel, close, surrender, or allow to lapse —
  examples include trusted-traveler memberships, optional agency accounts,
  Informed Delivery, emergency-alert profiles, donor registration, and voter
  registration if the subject elects cancellation.
- **REDUCE:** the access point or disclosure can be limited, but the agency's
  underlying record remains — close the portal account, remove optional contact
  and payment data, revoke connected access, correct errors, use a freeze/lock
  where offered, or disenroll prospectively after a consequences review.
- **STAY:** tax, court/probation, Selective Service while it applies, driver and
  vehicle duties while driving/owning, jury/census obligations, and regulated
  licensing while practicing. Name the real remedy — completion, correction,
  confidentiality, sealing, or properly ending the triggering status — and do
  not package it as an opt-out.
- **UNKNOWN:** discover enrollment first from the subject's own accounts,
  documents, and confirmations. Never infer from veteran status, age, or a
  public listing alone that he is in VA, benefits, trusted-traveler, or a state
  health registry.

Every proposed government exit gets a one-line consequence statement before it
is filed: what access, benefit, credential, convenience, or record will change;
what historical data the agency will retain; and how re-entry works. Account
closure and record deletion are reported as different outcomes.

### 8. Regulator and enforcement complaints

If a holder ignores a valid request, misses its verified response period,
demands excessive verification, refuses without a recognized basis, or re-lists
data in violation of a confirmed suppression:

1. Send a completion/second-notice packet citing the original filing, date,
   confirmation, requested remedy, and evidence of non-compliance.
2. If unresolved, prepare a complaint for the correct authority — for example
   the state attorney general or state privacy regulator, the California Privacy
   Protection Agency where applicable, the FTC, or the CFPB for consumer-reporting
   problems.
3. Preserve the complaint number and response in the case record. A regulator
   complaint is pressure and an enforcement referral, not a guarantee of removal.

## Filing packet standard

Every packet contains, as applicable:

- subject name and minimal matching identifiers;
- holder legal/business name and privacy contact/channel;
- specific record or URL to be acted on;
- remedy requested in one sentence;
- legal or policy basis, phrased only as specifically as verified;
- evidence exhibit (listing screenshot, URL, disclosure excerpt, or prior filing);
- authorization/authorized-agent statement where required;
- delivery method and proof of sending;
- response deadline verified from the current official source, plus the date the
  case enters follow-up;
- requested confirmation language: what was deleted, suppressed, corrected,
  opted out, or retained under an exception, and for how long.

No packet may claim a law, deadline, exemption, or residency the case record does
not support. If the basis is the holder's voluntary privacy process, say that.

## Case tracking fields

Extend `footprint/wipe-log.md` or the case register with:

`case_id | holder | record/URL | jurisdiction | instrument | legal/policy basis | date filed | delivery proof | confirmation/reference | response due verified from | outcome | exception claimed | follow-up date | regulator/counsel escalation | recheck date`

Sensitive verification documents are referenced by a restricted packet ID only.
The ordinary log contains no ID images or full government identifiers.

## Follow-through ladder

1. Filed and awaiting response.
2. Reminder/completion notice after the verified response period.
3. Appeal or supplemental evidence where the holder provides one.
4. Regulator complaint packet.
5. Counsel brief for litigation, sealing, injunction, or contested public record.
6. Recheck on the maintenance cadence; reopen automatically if the record returns.

## Deliverable per case

- filings sent, grouped by holder;
- confirmations and reference numbers;
- records confirmed removed, suppressed, corrected, frozen, or de-indexed;
- denials and the exact exception or reason given;
- regulator complaints and counsel escalations;
- legal-residue docket — what remains, why the ordinary paperwork cannot remove
  it, and the real next channel;
- next recheck date.
